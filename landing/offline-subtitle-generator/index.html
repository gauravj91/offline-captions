What an AI subtitle generator does, and where cloud tools stop

A subtitle generator from video listens to the speech, writes it out word by word with timings, and gives you subtitles: a file your editor or YouTube reads, or captions drawn into the picture. Most tools that call themselves an AI subtitle generator run in the cloud. You upload the video, wait, and pay by the minute, the credit or the month. An offline subtitle generator runs the same kind of model on your own machine. There is nothing to upload, no credits to run down, and no account between you and the file.

Make subtitles in three steps

1

Open the video

Drag in an MP4 or MOV. The speech model downloads the first time; after that, transcription runs on your PC without internet.

2

Review the flagged words

Words the AI is unsure about are highlighted yellow, so you fix those instead of re-reading everything. Fix a name once and the dictionary remembers it on every clip.

3

Export

SRT or VTT for YouTube and editors, or an MP4 with captions burned in at your video's resolution. You get the file and the video from one transcription.

How fast? In our benchmark the English engine (NVIDIA Parakeet) transcribed at 17.5x real time on a 2-core cloud CPU, about 3 to 4 seconds per minute of footage. That is transcription only; rendering the captioned MP4 takes extra time that depends on your PC. The longest video we have captioned in one run so far is about 20 minutes.

FAQ

Does it really work without internet?

Yes. Offline Captions downloads its speech model once, then transcribes and renders on your computer with no connection. Only licence activation needs the internet.

Is my video uploaded anywhere?

No. Video and audio stay on your machine. There is no cloud transcription step to opt out of.

Is there a free AI subtitle generator?

Yes. The Offline Captions web version is free for clips up to 5 minutes, with no signup, and exports MP4, SRT, VTT and TXT. Free desktop tools such as Subtitle Edit also make SRT files offline.

Can it subtitle long videos?

The desktop app has no video-length cap or credits; the longest we have captioned in one run so far is about 20 minutes. The free web version stops at 5 minutes per clip.

Does it work on Mac?

Windows 10 and 11 today. The Mac version is coming soon; the free web version already works in a browser on a Mac.

Which languages are supported?

English (NVIDIA Parakeet), Hinglish in Roman script, and other languages through Whisper. Translation is not included yet.

How accurate is it?

On our 28-clip English benchmark, the default engine scored a 7.6% word error rate (6.2% normalised). Unsure words are flagged yellow so you can fix them fast.
